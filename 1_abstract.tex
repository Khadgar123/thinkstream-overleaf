\begin{abstract}
Streaming video assistants must reason while a stream unfolds, answering from observed prefixes rather than completed clips.
This setting is challenging because frames arrive continuously, queries arise asynchronously, future frames are unavailable, and inference must remain low-latency.
Existing streaming Video-LLMs mainly learn when to respond from the active window, while compression methods keep long inputs tractable but may discard early evidence before later turns reveal its relevance.
We introduce Watch-Think-Interact (WTI), an end-to-end framework for long-horizon multi-turn streaming video reasoning with bounded context.
As the stream unfolds, WTI maintains a chunk-level thinking state and learns to stay silent, answer, or recall time-bounded evidence outside the active visual window.
When context reaches its limit, WTI rewrites observed history into compact time-indexed memory lines, allowing reasoning to continue over potentially unbounded streams.
To train this behavior, we construct WTI-82K and combine supervised initialization with Stream-GDPO to optimize correctness, timing, evidence use, and long-range history maintenance.
Experiments show that WTI achieves state-of-the-art streaming performance, reaching 83.3\% on StreamingBench and 73.6\% on OVO-Bench while preserving competitive long-video understanding across benchmarks.
\end{abstract}
